Physical AI · platform two

A neural network made of magnets

A weight is the magnetic state of a device, and the sum is performed by Kirchhoff's law on a wire. Not by interference, and not by a processor. This repository works out how precisely such a thing would have to be built.

01

The weight

A magnetic state, read as a conductance

In a magnetic tunnel junction two magnetic layers sandwich a thin barrier. When their magnetisations point the same way the device conducts comparatively well; when they oppose, it conducts less. The stored value is an orientation, and the circuit reads it as a conductance.

Domain-wall devices are the same idea with a position instead of an orientation, which lets them hold more than two distinguishable states. They are one model with a knob — states per weight — rather than two separate machines, which is why this repository builds one crossbar and parameterises it.

02

The sum

Kirchhoff's law does the arithmetic

Arrange the devices in a grid. Drive the rows with voltages, and each device passes a current set by its own conductance. Every device on a column dumps its current into the same wire, and the wire adds them — because that is what a wire does.

A matrix-vector multiply therefore costs one settling time and no instructions. The multiply-accumulate is linear; whatever nonlinearity the network needs sits in the periphery, in the converter and whatever follows it. That is a real difference from an optical network, where the detector supplies the nonlinearity for free, and this project intends to characterise the difference rather than argue from it.

03

The deliverable

One row, in a table that compares media

This is the second platform in a series. The first computes with light. One example of computation-by-physics is an anecdote; a second, in an unrelated medium, is what makes it a class — but only if the two can be put in the same table.

They can, because any analog tolerance normalises against the device's own operating range, and the log of that ratio is a bit depth. So this repository owes exactly one row: what a weight physically is, what performs the sum, the accuracy on the shared task, which error source binds first, and the precision that source demands in effective bits — alongside energy and latency per inference.

Every as-built magnitude is either cited or marked UNSOURCED and shown as such. Where a value cannot be sourced, no margin is claimed against it and the column is left out rather than estimated.

04

Status

What exists today, stated plainly

The comparable core is complete and the row is reported. The ideal array scores 0.7345 on the shared task. Under device error, conductance variation binds first, at 4.84 effective bits, ahead of resolvable states and IR drop. The array is 36×10 logical — 720 devices in differential pairs — and that size is stated because IR drop grows with it.

Delivered precision, energy per inference and latency are UNSOURCED, so no margin is claimed and that column is omitted rather than estimated. Array read power is 1.577 µW, array only: it excludes the sense amplifiers, the ADC and every digital stage after them, and the periphery frequently dominates an analog accelerator's energy. A figure that omits it quietly is not comparable to one that does not.

Not built, deliberately: error sources beyond the first three, an array-size sweep, and the spin-torque oscillator — which is complementary and stays out of the comparison table on purpose. None of them blocks the row.

What this is

A spintronic crossbar simulator and its device-error model: the ideal design in Python, handed one way to an as-built error model in MATLAB.

How numbers are treated

Cited, or marked UNSOURCED and surfaced. A confident wrong number costs more than an admitted hole.
